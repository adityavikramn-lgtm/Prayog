1. What is a fraction?

A fraction tells us how many equal parts we have out of the whole.

38 → 3 parts shaded, out of 8 equal parts

Numerator (top) = parts we take. Denominator (bottom) = equal parts in one whole.

Remember The parts must be equal. A pizza cut into 4 unequal pieces is not quarters!

2. Equal (equivalent) fractions

Different fractions can show the same amount:

12
24
48

To make an equal fraction, multiply (or divide) the top and the bottom by the same number:

12 = 1×22×2 = 24    1824 = 18÷624÷6 = 34 (simplest form)

3. Comparing fractions

  • Same bottom: bigger top wins. 57 > 37
  • Same top: smaller bottom wins (fewer, bigger pieces). 13 > 15
  • Different tops and bottoms: cross-multiply.

34 ? 57 → 3 × 7 = 21,  5 × 4 = 20 → 34 > 57

4. Adding and subtracting

Same bottom: add the tops, keep the bottom.

27 + 37 = 57

Different bottoms: first make the bottoms the same (use the LCM), then add.

1 LCM of 2 and 3 is 6
2 12 = 36,   13 = 26
3 36 + 26 = 56

Common mistake 12 + 13 is not 25. Never add the bottoms! Check: 12 alone is already more than 25.

5. More than one whole

When the top is bigger than the bottom, we have more than one whole:

74 = 1 34 (4 quarters make 1 whole, 3 quarters left)
