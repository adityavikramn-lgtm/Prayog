Work done dragging a block over a rough dome

Class notes · step by step, from zero · matches the video lesson
The question. A hemisphere of radius RRR is fixed on the ground. Its surface is rough, with coefficient of friction μ\muμ. Using a string, a person pulls a small block of mass mmm very slowly along the surface, from the ground all the way up to the top of the hemisphere. Find the work done by the person.

What we know

  • Radius of the dome: RRR
  • Mass of the block: mmm
  • Friction coefficient: μ\muμ
  • The block moves slowly
  • Find: WpersonW_{\text{person}}Wperson​

The plan

  1. Draw every force on the block.
  2. Slowly ⇒ forces balance ⇒ the pull TTT.
  3. TTT keeps changing, so add up tiny pieces: W=∫T dsW = \int T\,dsW=∫Tds.
  4. Check the answer with energy.

1. What is work?

A force does work when it moves something. If the force is at angle θ\thetaθ to the motion, only the part along the motion counts:

W=F scos⁡θW = F\,s\cos\thetaW=Fscosθ

Here the pull changes as the block climbs, so we cut the path into tiny pieces and add them up:

dW=F dsW=∫F dsdW = F\,ds \qquad W = \int F\,dsdW=FdsW=∫Fds

2. The forces on the block

  • Weight mgmgmg: straight down.
  • Normal force NNN: the dome pushes back, at right angles to the surface, out along the radius.
  • Friction f=μNf = \mu Nf=μN: fights the motion, so it points down the slope.
  • Pull TTT: the string lies along the surface, so it pulls up the slope.
Remember: slowly means no acceleration. So the forces balance in every direction, and the kinetic energy never changes (ΔKE=0\Delta KE = 0ΔKE=0).

4. Splitting the weight (the step most people get wrong)

  1. The radius and the surface always meet at a right angle.
  2. The radius makes φ\varphiφ with the ground. Turn both lines by 90°: radius → surface, ground → vertical. The angle stays the same.
  3. So mgmgmg makes φ\varphiφ with the surface, and 90∘−φ90^\circ - \varphi90∘−φ with the radius.

5. Balance the forces

Along the radius (out = in). TTT and fff lie along the surface, so they don't count:

N=mgsin⁡φ⇒f=μN=μmgsin⁡φN = mg\sin\varphi \quad\Rightarrow\quad f = \mu N = \mu mg\sin\varphiN=mgsinφ⇒f=μN=μmgsinφ

Along the surface (up = down). NNN is across the surface, so it doesn't count:

T=mgcos⁡φ+μmgsin⁡φT = mg\cos\varphi + \mu mg\sin\varphiT=mgcosφ+μmgsinφ
Careful: NNN is not mgmgmg. It changes along the dome, so friction changes too.
The pull for the worked example (2 kg, μ = 0.5). It is not largest at the bottom! (See P5.)

7. What the answer means

  • mgRmgRmgR = weight × height: the work to lift the block by RRR. The shape of the path doesn't matter.
  • μmgR\mu mgRμmgR = work against friction. This RRR is the horizontal distance, not the curved length.
  • Smooth dome (μ=0\mu = 0μ=0): W=mgRW = mgRW=mgR. Makes sense.
Handy rule: when you drag something slowly over any curved surface, friction's work is μmg×horizontal distance\mu mg \times \text{horizontal distance}μmg×horizontal distance.
