1. Why do we need a standard unit?

Riya and Arjun measured the same 1.6 m table with their handspans:


They got different numbers, but neither is wrong: Arjun's hand is smaller. Handspans, footspans, cubits and steps change from person to person. So everyone agreed on standard units that are the same everywhere.

Remember The SI unit of length is the metre (m). "SI" means the International System of Units, used by the whole world.

Big unit → small unit: multiply.   Small unit → big unit: divide.

Example 2.5 m = 2.5 × 100 = 250 cm.   340 cm = 340 ÷ 100 = 3.4 m.

3. How to measure correctly

  • Place the scale along the length, touching the object.
  • Start from the zero mark.
  • Keep your eye straight above the mark you read. Looking from the side gives a wrong reading.

Broken ruler? Start from any full mark and subtract:

Common mistake Reading the end mark (9.5 cm) as the length when the object starts at 2 cm.

4. Measuring a curved line

Lay a thread carefully along the curve, mark where it ends, then straighten the thread and measure it with a ruler.

Quick summary

  • Handspans and steps are not reliable: they differ from person to person.
  • SI unit of length: metre (m). 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm.
  • Measure from zero, scale along the object, eye straight above the mark.
