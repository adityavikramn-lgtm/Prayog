Electric Charge & Coulomb's Law

class notes · Adithya Vikram

1. Electric charge

  • Charge = basic property of matter (like mass). It's what causes electric force.
  • Only 2 types: positive (+) and negative (−). Names given by Franklin, just labels.
  • SI unit → coulomb (C). Small units: 1 µC = 10−6 C, 1 nC = 10−9 C.
the golden rule ↑

Where does charge come from?

  • atom = nucleus (protons +, neutrons 0) + electrons (−)
  • e = 1.6 × 10−19 C (charge of electron is −e, proton is +e)
  • me = 9.1 × 10−31 kg, mp = 1.67 × 10−27 kg
  • gains e⁻ → −ve    loses e⁻ → +ve  (only electrons move!)
Careful! +ve body has LOST electrons. It did not gain protons.

2. Properties of charge

(a) Additive → it's a scalar

Total charge = algebraic sum. e.g. +3 C, −5 C, +1 C → total = −1 C

(b) Quantised

q = ± n e     (n = 1, 2, 3, …)
you can never have 'half an electron' of charge
Q1. A body has charge −3.2 µC. How many extra electrons?
Soln:
  1. n = q / e
  2. n = 3.2 × 10−61.6 × 10−19
  3. = 2 × 10−6 + 19 = 2 × 1013
⇒ n = 2 × 1013 electrons

(c) Conserved

Charge can't be created or destroyed, only transferred. Glass + silk: glass loses exactly as many e⁻ as silk gains.

(d) Invariant

Charge doesn't change with speed (mass does at very high speed).

+, − or 0always +

same at any speedchanges near speed of light

3. Conductors vs insulators

  • Conductors: free electrons → charge moves easily. Metals, human body, earth.
  • Insulators: electrons tightly bound → charge stays where you put it. Glass, plastic, rubber, silk.
  • Earthing: connect to Earth. Earth is huge, so it can give/take any charge.
★ IMP Charge given to a conductor spreads on its outer surface.

4. Ways of charging

(i) Friction

  • glass + silk → glass +, silk −
  • ebonite + fur → ebonite −, fur +
  • both get equal & opposite charge

(ii) Conduction (touching)

charged body touches neutral one → neutral one gets same sign charge.

(iii) Induction (no touching!)

★ IMP Induction gives OPPOSITE sign, and the rod loses nothing.
Careful! Remove earth FIRST, then the rod. Otherwise the e⁻ go back to earth → neutral.

5. Coulomb's law (1785)

Force between 2 point charges:

  • ∝ product of charges (q1q2)
  • ∝ 1 / r2 (inverse square)
  • acts along the line joining them
F = k q1 q2r2     k = 14πε0 = 9 × 109 N m2/C2
ε0 = 8.85 × 10−12 C2/N m2 (permittivity of free space)
  • double one charge → F doubles
  • double r → F becomes ¼ (NOT ½!)
  • put signs in: answer + → repulsion, − → attraction
  • valid for point charges at rest (electro-statics)

Vector form

F21 = k q1q2r3 r12
force on 2 due to 1; r12 points from 1 to 2. And F12 = − F21
Q2. Force between +3 µC and −2 µC, 30 cm apart (vacuum)?
Soln:
  1. q1 = 3×10−6, q2 = 2×10−6, r = 0.3 m
  2. F = 9×109 × 3×10−6 × 2×10−6(0.3)2
  3. = 54 × 10−30.09 = 0.6 N
  4. opposite signs → attraction
⇒ F = 0.600 N (attractive)

6. In a medium

Fmedium = FvacuumK     ε = K ε0
K = dielectric constant (relative permittivity)
  • vacuum K = 1, air ≈ 1, water ≈ 80, metal → ∞

Same charges as above, now in a liquid with K = 4 → F = 0.6 / 4 = 0.150 N

8. Superposition

Each pair acts independently. Net force = vector sum:

Fnet = F1 + F2 + F3 + …

Method: (1) size of each force by Coulomb  (2) direction: away if like, towards if unlike  (3) add as vectors

Q4. A = +2 µC (x = 0), B = −1 µC (x = 10 cm), C = +4 µC (x = 30 cm). Net force on B?
Soln:
  1. from A: 9×109×2×10−6×1×10−6(0.1)² = 1.8 N → towards A
  2. from C: 9×109×4×10−6×1×10−6(0.2)² = 0.9 N → towards C
  3. opposite directions → 1.8 − 0.9
⇒ 0.900 N towards A
6

Four charges on a square

Q5. 4 equal charges q = 1 µC on a square of side 10 cm. Net force on one?
Soln:
  1. 2 neighbours: each F1 = kq²/a² = 0.900 N, at 90° → √2 F1
  2. diagonal one: distance √2 a → F1/2, along the same diagonal
  3. total = (√2 + ½) F1
⇒ F ≈ 1.72 N along the diagonal
Trick Equilateral triangle (side a, equal q): F = √3 · kq²/a² on each.

9. Null point (net force = 0)

★ IMP like → between, near smaller  |  unlike → outside, beyond smaller  |  test charge cancels out
Q6. +2 µC and +8 µC, 30 cm apart. Null point?
Soln:
  1. let x from 2 µC → 2x² = 8(30 − x)²
  2. √ both sides: 1x = 230 − x → 30 − x = 2x
  3. x = 10
⇒ 10.0 cm from +2 µC
Q7. +4 µC and −1 µC, 30 cm apart. Null point?
Soln:
  1. unlike → outside, beyond −1 µC. Let x beyond it.
  2. 4(30 + x)² = 1x² → 2x = 30 + x
⇒ x = 30.0 cm beyond −1 µC (60 cm from +4 µC)

All 3 in equilibrium

q and 4q, distance d. Third charge Q = −4q/9 at d/3 from q.

(why? Q sits at the null point → d/3. Then for q: pull of Q = push of 4q → kq|Q|(d/3)² = kq·4qd² → |Q| = 4q/9, and Q must be −ve)

10. Touching identical spheres

each gets q1 + q22   (with signs!)
Q8. +8 µC and −2 µC identical spheres touch, then back to same distance. F'/F ?
Soln:
  1. each = (8 − 2)/2 = +3 µC
  2. before ∝ 8 × 2 = 16 (attract)
  3. after ∝ 3 × 3 = 9 (repel)
⇒ F'/F = 9/16, attraction → repulsion

11. Hanging charged balls

  1. vertical: T cos θ = mg
  2. horizontal: T sin θ = F
  3. divide → T cancels
tan θ = Fmg = kq2r2 mg

e.g. m = 0.9 g, q = 0.1 µC, r = 10 cm → F = 9×10−3 N = mg → tan θ = 1 → θ = 45.0°

9
★ IMP In a liquid (density σ, dielectric K): same angle if   K = ρρ − σ   (ρ = ball density). e.g. ρ = 1600, σ = 800 → K = 2.00

12. Max force trick

Q split into q and (Q − q): F ∝ q(Q − q) → d/dq = 0 → q = Q/2

Common mistakes ✗

  • not converting µC → C and cm → m
  • forgetting to square r
  • adding forces like numbers (they're vectors!)
  • "bigger charge feels bigger force" → NO, both feel the same (Newton's 3rd)
  • null point on wrong side
  • sharing charge without signs (+8, −2 → +3 each, not +5)
  • in a medium force is ÷ K, not × K
