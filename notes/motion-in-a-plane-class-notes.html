1. Scalars and vectors

  • Scalar: only size (magnitude). e.g. mass, time, distance, speed, work, energy.
  • Vector: size + direction, and adds by the triangle law. e.g. displacement, velocity, acceleration, force.
  • Current has direction but is NOT a vector (it doesn't add by triangle law). Classic trap.

Types of vectors

  • Equal: same magnitude, same direction (can be at different places).
  • Negative: same magnitude, opposite direction. A + (−A) = 0.
  • Zero / null 0: magnitude 0, direction not defined. e.g. displacement after a full round.
  • Unit vector A = A|A|: magnitude 1, only gives direction. i, j, k along x, y, z.
  • Position vector r: from origin to the point. Displacement = r2 − r1.
  • Collinear (parallel/antiparallel) and coplanar (in one plane).

2. Adding vectors

R = √A² + B² + 2AB cosθ     tan α = B sinθA + B cosθ
θ = angle between A and B (tail to tail), α = angle of R from A

Special cases (learn these!)

θRmeaning
0°A + Bmaximum
90°√A² + B²perpendicular
180°|A − B|minimum
|A − B| ≤ R ≤ A + B
any third vector outside this range can never be the resultant
Shortcut Two equal vectors A at angle θ: R = 2A cos(θ/2), and R bisects the angle. R = A when θ = 120°; R = A√2 at 90°; R = A√3 at 60°.

Subtraction

A − B = A + (−B).   |A − B| = √A² + B² − 2AB cosθ

  • Addition is commutative and associative; subtraction is not commutative.
  • Polygon law: many vectors head-to-tail; closing side (start → end) is the resultant. Closed polygon ⇒ resultant 0.

3. Resolution and components

Split A along x and y:

Aₓ = A cosθ,   Aᵧ = A sinθ
A = √Aₓ² + Aᵧ²,   tanθ = AᵧAₓ
A = Aₓi + Aᵧj + Azk,    |A| = √Aₓ² + Aᵧ² + Az²

Direction cosines: cos α = AₓA, cos β = AᵧA, cos γ = AzA  ⇒  cos²α + cos²β + cos²γ = 1

Adding by components (the fastest way)

A + B = (Aₓ + Bₓ)i + (Aᵧ + Bᵧ)j + (Az + Bz)k. Add i with i, j with j, k with k.

★ IMP A vector can be resolved along any two directions, but components along perpendicular axes are the useful ones. Component along a direction = A cos(angle with that direction).

Multiplying by a number

  • λA: magnitude |λ|A; same direction if λ > 0, opposite if λ < 0.
  • Multiplying by a scalar with units (e.g. mass × velocity) gives a new physical quantity (momentum).
Shortcut To find a vector of magnitude k along A: k A = k A|A|.
e.g. A 10 N force at 37° to the x-axis: Fₓ = 10 cos37° = 10 × 45 = 8 N,   Fᵧ = 10 × 35 = 6 N.
e.g. A = 3i + 4j: |A| = √9 + 16 = 5,   A = 3i + 4j5,   angle with x = tan⁻¹43 ≈ 53.1° (the 37–53 triangle!).

4. Dot (scalar) product

A·B = AB cosθ = AₓBₓ + AᵧBᵧ + Az Bz
answer is a scalar
  • i·i = j·j = k·k = 1,   i·j = 0 ...
  • Angle between: cosθ = A·BAB.   A ⟂ B ⇔ A·B = 0.
  • Projection of A on B = A·BB.   Work W = F·s.
  • Commutative: A·B = B·A.

Cross (vector) product

|A × B| = AB sinθ,   direction ⟂ to both (right-hand rule)
A × B = (Aᵧ Bz − Az Bᵧ)i − (Aₓ Bz − Az Bₓ)j + (AₓBᵧ − AᵧBₓ)k
write as a 3×3 determinant with i, j, k on top
  • i×j = k, j×k = i, k×i = j (cyclic); reverse order ⇒ minus sign. i×i = 0.
  • Not commutative: A × B = −B × A.   Parallel vectors ⇒ cross = 0.
  • |A × B| = area of the parallelogram; half of it = area of the triangle.
Shortcut Check your cross product: dot it with A and with B. Both must be 0. Also |A×B|² + (A·B)² = A²B².
Careful! NCERT keeps dot product in Work–Energy and cross product in Rotation, but JEE asks both with vectors, so learn them here.
e.g. Find λ so that (2i + λj) ⟂ (3i − 6j): dot = 6 − 6λ = 0 ⇒ λ = 1.
e.g. Area of the triangle formed by A = 2i + 3j − k and B = i − 2j + 2k: ½|A × B| = ½√90 ≈ 4.74 unit².
Shortcut Unit vector ⟂ to both A and B: n = ± A × B|A × B|.

5. Position, velocity, acceleration in a plane

r = xi + yj,   Δr = Δxi + Δyj
  • Average velocity vavg = ΔrΔt (direction of displacement).
  • Instantaneous velocity v = drdt = vₓi + vᵧj; vₓ = dxdt, vᵧ = dydt.
  • Velocity is always along the tangent to the path. Its angle: tanθ = vᵧvₓ.
  • Acceleration a = dvdt = d²rdt². It can point anywhere, not only along v.
  • Speed = |v| = √vₓ² + vᵧ². Average speed (distance/time) ≥ |average velocity|.
★ IMP a ∥ v ⇒ speed changes, path straight.   a ⟂ v ⇒ only direction changes (speed constant).   In general a has both parts.

6. Constant acceleration in a plane

v = u + at,    r = r0 + ut + ½at²

The x and y motions are independent. Use the 1-D equations separately:

x = uₓt + ½aₓt²,   y = uᵧt + ½aᵧt²,   vₓ² = uₓ² + 2aₓx ...
Shortcut Given r(t) or x(t), y(t): differentiate once for v, twice for a. Eliminate t to get the path y(x).
e.g. x = 3t, y = 4t − 5t² (SI). Then vₓ = 3, vᵧ = 4 − 10t, so at t = 0 the speed is 5 m/s at 53.1°; aₓ = 0, aᵧ = -10 m/s². Put t = x/3: y = 43x − 59x², a parabola. It's just a projectile!
e.g. u = 2i m/s, a = 2j m/s², t = 3 s: v = 2i + 6j m/s,   r = 6i + 9j m (start at origin).

7. Relative velocity

vAB = vA − vB
velocity of A as seen by B
  • vBA = −vAB.
  • Same direction: |vAB| = vA − vB. Opposite: vA + vB. At angle θ: √vA² + vB² − 2vA vB cosθ.

Rain–man problems

Rain falls vertically at vr. You walk at vm. Rain relative to you:

vrm = vr − vm
tanθ = vmvr
θ from the vertical, tilt umbrella forward

Rain seems to hit you at √vr² + vm².

Shortcut "Rain appears vertical" means the horizontal parts cancel: vrain,x = vman. Draw the velocity triangle, then just read off the angle.
Careful! Relative velocity is about what one observer sees. Always write vAB = vA − vB with signs/directions first, then put numbers.
e.g. Car A goes east at 20 m/s, car B goes north at 15 m/s. vAB = 20i − 15j: |vAB| = √400 + 225 = 25 m/s, at 36.9° south of east. (B sees A moving away "south-east".)
Shortcut Closest approach of two moving bodies: go to the frame of one of them. The other then moves in a straight line with vrel; the least distance is the perpendicular from the first body to that line.

8. River–boat problems

River width d, flow vr, boat speed in still water vb, heading at θ with the straight-across line (upstream +).

time t = dvb cosθ,   drift x = (vr − vb sinθ)·t

① Shortest time: head straight across (θ = 0).

tmin = dvb,   drift = vr dvb

② Shortest path (zero drift): aim upstream.

sinθ = vrvb
t = d√vb² − vr²
★ IMP Zero drift is possible only if vb > vr. If vb < vr, the drift is least when sinθ = vbvr (θ measured from the straight-across line, towards upstream).
Shortcut Shortest time never depends on the river speed: the river only carries you sideways. Aim straight, multiply time by vr for the drift.

Same idea for an aeroplane in wind: velocity relative to ground = velocity relative to air + wind velocity.

9. Projectile motion (ground to ground)

Only gravity acts (no air resistance). x: uniform motion, y: uniformly accelerated (−g). uₓ = u cosθ, uᵧ = u sinθ.

x = (u cosθ)t,    y = (u sinθ)t − ½gt²
Trajectory: y = x tanθ − g x²2u²cos²θ  =  x tanθ (1 − xR)
a parabola
Time of flightMax heightRange
T = 2u sinθgH = u² sin²θ2gR = u² sin2θg
  • Velocity at time t: vₓ = u cosθ (constant), vᵧ = u sinθ − gt; v = √vₓ² + vᵧ², tanφ = vᵧvₓ.
  • At the top: vᵧ = 0, v = u cosθ, v ⟂ g. Time to top = T/2.
  • Lands with the same speed u at angle θ below the horizontal.
e.g. Path y = x − x²20 (SI, g = 10). Compare with y = x tanθ − gx²2u²cos²θ: tanθ = 1 ⇒ θ = 45°; 102u²(½) = 120 ⇒ u = √200 ≈ 14.1 m/s. Range (y = 0): R = 20 m,   H = R/4 = 5 m.

Projectile: key results & shortcuts

  • Max range at θ = 45°: Rmax = u²g; then H = Rmax4. e.g. u = 20 m/s: Rmax = 40 m, H = 10 m.
  • Complementary angles θ and 90° − θ give the same range (e.g. 15° & 75° at 20 m/s: 20 m each).
  • For those two: H1 + H2 = u²2g,   R = 4√H₁H₂,   T1T2 = 2Rg.
  • H and R: HR = tanθ4 ⇒ tanθ = 4HR.   H = R ⇒ θ = tan⁻¹4.   Also HT² = g8.
  • Reaches height h twice at t1, t2: t1 + t2 = T,   t1t2 = 2hg.
  • KE at the top = K cos²θ (K = initial KE). Momentum change from launch to landing = 2mu sinθ (downward).
  • Radius of curvature at the top: r = (u cosθ)²g; at launch: r = u²g cosθ.
  • Angle between v and a: 90° + θ at launch, 90° at top, 90° − θ at landing.
Shortcut For any "same u, two angles, same range" question: the angles add to 90°. Write R = 4√(H₁H₂) or T₁T₂ = 2R/g and you're done in one line.
Careful! These shortcuts need same launch and landing level and no air resistance. From a height or on an incline they change.

10. Projectile from a height

Thrown horizontally with speed u from height h:

T = √2h/g,   x = u√2h/g
vᵧ = gT = √2gh,   v = √u² + 2gh

Path: y = g x²2u² (measured down from the top).

Thrown at θ from height h: use −h = (u sinθ)t − ½gt², solve the quadratic for t (take the + root), then x = (u cosθ)t.

★ IMP Time of fall depends only on h, not on u. A ball dropped and a ball thrown horizontally from the same height land together.

Projectile on an inclined plane

Incline angle α, launched at θ to the horizontal (up the incline). Take axes along and ⟂ to the incline: g has parts g sinα (along) and g cosα (⟂).

T = 2u sin(θ − α)g cosα
R = 2u² sin(θ − α) cosθg cos²α
Rmax, up = u²g(1 + sinα) at θ = 45° + α/2;   Rmax, down = u²g(1 − sinα)
Shortcut Hits the incline at right angles ⇔ the velocity along the incline is zero at landing: u cos(θ−α) = g sinα · T. Use the incline axes and it's 1-D.

11. Uniform circular motion

  • Angular displacement Δθ (rad), angular velocity ω = dθdt,   ω = 2πT = 2πf.
  • Speed v = ωr (constant), but the velocity changes direction ⇒ there is an acceleration.
ac = v²r = ω²r = 4π²rT²    (towards the centre)
★ IMP In uniform circular motion speed is constant, but velocity and acceleration are not constant vectors (their directions keep turning). |a| stays the same.
  • v = ω × r; v ⟂ r, a ∥ −r.
  • In one full turn: displacement = 0, average velocity = 0, distance = 2πr.
  • Over half a turn: |Δv| = 2v; over angle θ: |Δv| = 2v sin(θ/2).

12. Non-uniform circular motion

aₜ = dvdt = αr (along v),   ac = v²r,   a = √ac² + aₜ²
angle of a with v: tanφ = ac / aₜ

Radius of curvature of any path: r = v²a⊥ (a⊥ = part of a perpendicular to v). For y(x): r = (1 + y′²)3/2|y″|.

Shortcut Projectile at the top: a⊥ = g, v = u cosθ ⇒ r = u²cos²θ / g. Same rule works at any point of any curve.

Common mistakes

  • Using θ = angle between head and tail instead of tail-to-tail in R = √(A² + B² + 2AB cosθ).
  • Forgetting that range formulas need launch and landing at the same level.
  • Taking velocity at the top as zero. Only vᵧ = 0; v = u cosθ.
  • Writing vAB = vB − vA (wrong order).
  • Saying "uniform circular motion has zero acceleration". It has v²/r towards the centre.

PYQ-pattern questions with solutions

Original questions written in the style of recurring JEE Main / Advanced and NCERT themes (not copied from papers). Every answer is computed and checked by code.

Q1. JEE Main pattern · vectors Two vectors of magnitude 3 and 5 make 60° with each other. Find the resultant and its angle with the first vector.
Soln:
  1. R = √(9 + 25 + 2·3·5·cos60°) = √49 = 7
  2. tan α = 5 sin60° / (3 + 5 cos60°) = 4.33 / 5.5 ⇒ α ≈ 38.2°
⇒ R = 7, α ≈ 38.2°
Q2. JEE Main pattern · equal vectors Two forces of equal magnitude F have a resultant of magnitude F. Find the angle between them.
Soln:
  1. Equal vectors: R = 2F cos(θ/2) = F
  2. cos(θ/2) = ½ ⇒ θ/2 = 60°
⇒ θ = 120°
Q3. JEE Main pattern · dot & cross A = 2i + 3j − k, B = i − 2j + 2k. Find A·B, the angle between them, and A × B.
Soln:
  1. A·B = 2 − 6 − 2 = -6; |A| = √14, |B| = 3
  2. cosθ = −6 / (3√14) ⇒ θ ≈ 122°
  3. A × B = (3·2 − (−1)(−2))i − (2·2 − (−1)(1))j + (2(−2) − 3·1)k = 4i − 5j − 7k
  4. Check: (A×B)·A = 8 − 15 + 7 = 0 ✓
⇒ A·B = -6, θ ≈ 122°, |A×B| = √90 ≈ 9.49
Q4. JEE Main pattern · calculus A particle moves with r = 2t²i + (3t − t²)j m. Find its speed at t = 1 s and its acceleration.
Soln:
  1. v = 4ti + (3 − 2t)j ⇒ at t = 1: 4i + j
  2. speed = √17 ≈ 4.12 m/s
  3. a = 4i − 2j (constant), |a| = √20
⇒ 4.12 m/s; a = 4i − 2j, |a| ≈ 4.47 m/s²

Q5. JEE Main pattern · projectile A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 30° above the horizontal (g = 10). Find T, H and R.
Soln:
  1. uₓ = 20 cos30° = 17.3, uᵧ = 10 m/s
  2. T = 2uᵧ/g = 2 s;   H = uᵧ²/2g = 5 m
  3. R = u² sin60° / g = 400(0.866)/10 = 34.6 m
⇒ T = 2 s, H = 5 m, R = 34.6 m
Q6. JEE Main pattern · complementary angles Two balls are thrown with the same speed at 30° and 60° (u = 20 m/s). Their heights are H₁ and H₂. Show the range is 4√(H₁H₂).
Soln:
  1. H₁ = 5 m, H₂ = 15 m
  2. 4√(H₁H₂) = 4√75 = 34.6 m = R for both ✓
  3. Also H₁ + H₂ = 20 m = u²/2g ✓
⇒ R = 34.6 m
Q7. JEE Main pattern · horizontal projection A ball is thrown horizontally at 10 m/s from a 45 m high cliff. Find the time, the distance from the foot, and the velocity on landing.
Soln:
  1. T = √(2h/g) = √9 = 3 s; x = uT = 30 m
  2. vᵧ = gT = 30 m/s, v = √(100 + 900) ≈ 31.6 m/s
  3. angle below horizontal: tan⁻¹(30/10) ≈ 71.6°
⇒ 3 s, 30 m, 31.6 m/s at 71.6° below horizontal
Q8. JEE Main pattern · H vs R At what angle must a projectile be thrown so that its maximum height equals its range?
Soln:
  1. H/R = tanθ/4 = 1
  2. tanθ = 4
⇒ θ = tan⁻¹4 ≈ 76.0°

Q9. JEE Main pattern · river–boat A river is 100 m wide and flows at 3 m/s. A boat moves at 5 m/s in still water. (a) Least time to cross and the drift? (b) Direction and time to reach the point straight opposite?
Soln:
  1. (a) head straight across: t = 100/5 = 20 s; drift = 3 × 20 = 60 m
  2. (b) sinθ = 3/5 ⇒ θ ≈ 36.9° upstream from straight-across
  3. speed across = √(25 − 9) = 4 m/s ⇒ t = 100/4 = 25 s
⇒ (a) 20 s, 60 m; (b) 36.9° upstream, 25 s
Q10. JEE Main pattern · rain–man Rain falls vertically at 10 m/s. A man walks at 5 m/s. At what angle should he hold his umbrella?
Soln:
  1. vrain,man = vrain − vman
  2. tanθ = 5/10 ⇒ θ ≈ 26.6° from vertical, tilted forward
  3. rain seen at √125 ≈ 11.2 m/s
⇒ 26.6° forward from vertical
Q11. NCERT / JEE pattern · uniform circular A stone tied to a 0.5 m string is whirled at 2 revolutions per second. Find ω, v and the centripetal acceleration.
Soln:
  1. ω = 2πf = 4π ≈ 12.6 rad/s
  2. v = ωr = 2π ≈ 6.28 m/s
  3. a = ω²r = 8π² ≈ 79.0 m/s² (towards the centre)
⇒ ω ≈ 12.6 rad/s, v ≈ 6.28 m/s, a ≈ 79.0 m/s²
Q12. JEE Main pattern · non-uniform circular A particle on a circle of radius 2 m has speed 4 m/s, increasing at 3 m/s². Find its total acceleration.
Soln:
  1. ac = v²/r = 16/2 = 8 m/s²; aₜ = 3 m/s²
  2. a = √(64 + 9) = √73 ≈ 8.54 m/s²
  3. angle with v: tan⁻¹(8/3) ≈ 69.4°
⇒ 8.54 m/s² at 69.4° to the velocity

Q13. JEE Advanced pattern · incline A ball is thrown at 20 m/s at 75° to the horizontal, up an incline of 30°. Find the time of flight and the range along the incline. What angle would give the maximum range?
Soln:
  1. T = 2u sin(θ − α)/(g cosα) = 2·20·sin45°/(10 cos30°) ≈ 3.27 s
  2. R = 2u² sin(θ−α) cosθ/(g cos²α) = 2·400·(0.707)(0.259)/(10·0.75) ≈ 19.5 m
  3. max range at θ = 45° + α/2 = 60°: Rmax = u²/g(1 + sinα) ≈ 26.7 m
⇒ T ≈ 3.27 s, R ≈ 19.5 m; best angle 60°
Q14. JEE Main pattern · speed at top The speed of a projectile at its highest point is half its launch speed. Find the launch angle and the ratio of KE at the top to the initial KE.
Soln:
  1. u cosθ = u/2 ⇒ cosθ = ½
  2. KEtop / KE = cos²θ = ¼
⇒ θ = 60°, ratio 1 : 4
Q15. JEE Advanced pattern · radius of curvature Find the radius of curvature of the path at the highest point for u = 20 m/s at 60°.
Soln:
  1. At the top a⊥ = g and v = u cos60° = 10 m/s
  2. r = v²/g = 100/10
⇒ r = 10 m

Last-minute checklist

  • Resolve everything into x and y first. Most 2-D problems become two 1-D problems.
  • Projectile: horizontal velocity never changes; vertical is just free fall.
  • Same level? Use T, H, R formulas. Different level / incline? Use components or incline axes.
  • Relative velocity: draw the triangle vAB = vA − vB before any numbers.
  • Circle: ac = v²/r always; add aₜ if the speed changes.
